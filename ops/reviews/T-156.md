## Findings

F1 [P1, blocking] Visible tabs and rows do not match mouse hit coordinates — `crates/gear-board/src/frame.rs:127`, `crates/gear-board/src/frame.rs:152`, `crates/gear-board/src/lib.rs:1055`. Header inserts a separate projection row whenever a voyage exists and another row when the engine is down, but the handler always treats terminal row 4 as tabs. With the existing fixture the tabs are visibly on row 5: clicking Graph at x=12,y=5 does nothing; clicking the first lane at y=8 sets index 2 although only two lanes exist. Even with a four-row header, VIEW_ROW denotes the view heading, so the first data row is one row below the handler's index zero. Breaks v0.4.1's prototype keys/live-data sentence and STABLE normal mouse usability. Fix: in `header`, keep projection on the time row as the prototype does and keep warnings from shifting the tab row, then share actual tab/data-row origins between drawing and hit testing (about 25–40 lines). Ignore clicks outside actual data rows. Pass the active tab into the header and visibly mark it as in the prototype. Verify clicks at the rendered tab and both lane coordinates, with a voyage and with engine-down state.

F2 [P1, blocking] Frames ignore terminal dimensions and can scroll their own header and tabs away — `crates/gear-board/src/frame.rs:100`, `crates/gear-board/src/screen.rs:166`. Every graph task, needs-a-look item and unbounded done clause is printed, and the footer is appended to the body rather than placed at the terminal bottom. In an 80-column terminal even the 86-character footer wraps; with more tasks than screen rows the alternate buffer scrolls and the title/tabs disappear. WIDTH=120 also ignores the actual terminal. The prototype clips to the window and reserves its last row for the footer. Breaks the v0.4.1 frame requirement and STABLE normal terminal usability. Fix: read terminal width/height (with a deterministic injected size for tests), bound each displayed row to its available columns, reserve header/footer rows, and bound the body to that viewport; keep the selected row visible when navigating long views (roughly 50–80 lines in frame/run/draw). Verify an 80x24 screen and a graph longer than the viewport retain the tabs/footer and show the selected task.

F3 [P1, blocking] The panel is truncated to the underlying view and is appended after existing text — `crates/gear-board/src/frame.rs:378`, `crates/gear-board/src/frame.rs:384`, `crates/gear-board/src/lib.rs:1048`. `bottom` is capped by `lines.len()`, so a short graph cannot show its longer task details even on a tall terminal: the four-task graph shows only nine of 40 supplied panel rows. `pad_to` never truncates the background at `left`, so long underlying rows push the box past its intended column; the closing border can be absent when the panel reaches the end. There is also no prototype-style any-key close: Enter/Escape leave the panel open, and q exits the board. Breaks the requested overlay and useful lane/task details, hence v0.4.1 and STABLE. Fix: composite into a terminal-sized canvas, replace background cells starting at the panel's left edge, size the box against terminal space rather than body length, preserve its border/footer and provide access to longer panel content; consume a key to close an open panel before normal board handling (about 40–60 lines). Test a short graph with a longer panel, a long background row, and Enter then Escape returning to the same view.

F4 [P1, blocking] Down/j can move selection past the last row, disabling highlighting and Enter — `crates/gear-board/src/lib.rs:1043`. On the existing two-lane fixture, Down twice followed by Enter produces neither a selected lane nor a panel. Graph has the same issue; a live refresh shrinking the row set can also invalidate the index. The prototype clamps at n-1. Breaks the v0.4.1 selection/Enter requirement and STABLE keyboard usability. Fix: derive the selectable row count for the active tab, clamp after a rebuild and after movement, and handle zero rows explicitly (about 10–15 lines). Verify repeated Down at the last lane/task still opens that last row.

F5 [P1, blocking] Quitting enables mouse reporting instead of disabling it — `crates/gear-board/src/screen.rs:161`. `leave` emits `ESC[?1000h`; the public-run PTY reproduction observed that sequence and no `ESC[?1000l`. After a normal q, terminal clicks can send mouse escapes into the user's shell. Breaks STABLE normal-use terminal restoration. Fix: change the second mode sequence to `ESC[?1000l` (one character), and check the public entry/exit path disables both 1000 and 1006 while restoring the cursor and screen.

F6 [P2, blocking] The acceptance test reads partial frames and clicks an invisible tab position — `crates/gear-board/tests/it/board_contract.rs:694`, `crates/gear-board/tests/it/board_contract.rs:700`, `crates/gear-board/tests/it/board_contract.rs:707`. Only the initial read stops on the footer. Later reads stop at `○ queued`, `LANES`, and `left: `, contrary to the explicit poll-until-footer contract; unread frame tails can satisfy subsequent reads. The click is hardcoded at row 4 while this fixture draws its tabs on row 5, allowing the broken visible-coordinate behavior to pass. Left-arrow switching is not tested. This is a failed mechanical contract, and it masks F1. Fix: use a complete footer-terminated frame for every action, then assert within that frame; click the actual rendered tab/row positions, add Left and row-click/Enter checks, and seed/assert an active voyage, idle reason, quota reading and done text (about 35–50 test lines). Keep poll(POLLIN), no sleeps, under 10 s.

F7 [P1, blocking] The time bar uses completed-work pace instead of elapsed voyage time — `crates/gear-board/src/frame.rs:140`. `bar(measured_actual_min, measured_estimate_min, 24)` measures actual/estimated effort of completed tasks, while the adjacent text says elapsed of ETA. A voyage 60 minutes into a 12-hour ETA with one on-estimate completed task therefore displays a full bar rather than approximately 8%. This misstates the live voyage's time budget and violates the prototype's `used_m/eta_m` meaning and STABLE accuracy in normal use. Fix: parse the voyage ETA using the existing duration parsing convention and feed elapsed/ETA minutes to the bar; show an explicit unknown bar when either is unavailable (about 10–15 lines). Verify 60/720 gives two filled cells of 24 independently of completed-task pace.

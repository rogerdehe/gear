## F1 — the binary's stop (was missing)

`strings` on the installed native binary (`…/@openai/codex/node_modules/@openai/codex-darwin-arm64/vendor/aarch64-apple-darwin/bin/codex`, 0.164.0) — `command -v codex` resolves to the npm launcher, which carries no TUI text:

```
ve hit your usage limit.
ve hit your usage limit. Upgrade to Plus to continue using Codex (https://chatgpt.com/explore/plus),
ve hit your usage limit. Upgrade to Pro (https://chatgpt.com/explore/pro), visit https://chatgpt.com/settings/usage to purchase more credits
ve hit your usage limit. Visit https://chatgpt.com/settings/usage to purchase more credits
ve hit your usage limit. To get more access now, send a request to your admin
ve hit your usage limit for
```

The `ve` prefix is what `strings` keeps of `You’ve` — the apostrophe is U+2019, a non-ASCII byte, so the word before it is split off. Hence the marker `hit your usage limit`, which matches every form and both apostrophes.

| On screen | What it is |
|---|---|
| `You’ve hit your usage limit.` (+ upgrade / credits / admin / nothing) | the sentence the TUI prints when the plan is spent — **the normal-use case** |
| `Usage limit reached` over `Your included usage is exhausted. Choose an option below to continue.` | the modal |
| `Usage limit reached. You've reached your usage limit. Increase your limits to continue using codex.` | the same stop as an error line |
| `You've reached your workspace credit limit`, `Your workspace is out of credits`, `You hit your spend cap` | the workspace (enterprise) forms |

The warning is still not a marker: `⚠ weekly limit: only 3% left · resets at 3:30 AM on 1 Nov · /status`, `Heads up, you have less than 5% of your weekly limit left. Run /status for a breakdown.`, and the status-line items `Remaining usage on the weekly usage limit`, `Approaching rate limits`, `Goal hit usage limits (/goal resume)`.

## F2 — the move (was not wired)

The attempt-1 report said `on_quota.review` was parsed but never applied, and that `restart.rs` was lane-only. Both are fixed:

1. Two quota screens in a row (the profile's `confirm_rounds`) on an **idle** reviewer are a confirmed stop — a working agent with a stale limit line is never moved (the M-266 rule, now tested for reviewers).
2. `on_quota.review = "fallback"` picks the entry after the reviewer's current model in `[models].review`. Another CLI (Codex → OpenCode) is a cross-CLI restart: the seat leaves the doorbell's reach, the old agent is closed with the profile's exit keys, the new CLI starts in the same pane with the entry's model and the seat's worktree, and the startup model gate confirms the status bar before any work.
3. The job travels with the seat: `resume_delivery` hands the reviewer a **full review packet** for the same task and attempt (not the lane's `Continue T-n`, and not `open_attempts_of_seat`, which would find the writer's attempt) — the reviewer's new conversation did not see the old packet.
4. The seat is `Active` again in a new generation, the model is recorded, the quota counter resets, and the captain gets one information line (`restarted on opencode (opencode-go/deepseek-v4-pro)`).
5. The reviewer's verdict then settles the very attempt it was moved for: the test submits it through `gear_app::review::submit_review` and reads it back from the attempt's review record.
6. An exhausted review chain, or `on_quota.review = "wait"`: a temp seat has no `Parked` state, so it is flagged `NeedsCheck` and the captain is rung once with the task whose review waits.

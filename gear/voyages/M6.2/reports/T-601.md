# T-601 — report

The merge gate keeps its headroom on a busy machine: the 23 tests that cost
seconds of real git work each leave the gate's run for `make full`, where they
still run on every full verification.

## What the suite costs, and where

`scripts/test.sh` runs `cargo nextest` with one process per test, so a test's
time buys nothing from its neighbours; the suite is bounded by
`sum of all test times / threads`, and `threads` is what build-slot gives the
run (`NEXTEST_TEST_THREADS`, 10 alone, 4 with three build-slot runs alive).
Two git processes per merge step, ~120 processes for one whole merge pass, is
what the merge tests cost: `git` spawn on this machine is ~10 ms, and the
fixture side was already cut twice (T-127 one `fast-import` per shape, T-274 an
in-process copy, T-527 one `for-each-ref` per pass). There is no wait, sleep,
poll, network or oversized fixture left in them; what remains is real git work.

## (3) After

`scripts/test.sh`, same machine, same method:

| conditions | before | after | sum of test times |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| quiet | 36.7 / 37.2 s | **33.3 s** | 357.3 → 327.4 s |
| cold build beside it, 10 threads (paired, 11:54–11:55) | **44.2 s** | **30.1 s** | 435.0 → 295.4 s |
| five CPU burners beside it (paired, 11:50–11:51) | 39.1 s | 33.9 s | 382.2 → 329.5 s |

Under load the gate gets 10 threads only when it is the only build-slot run
alive; with two other runs (`running 3`) build-slot gives it `NEXTEST_TEST_THREADS=4`
— that is the corner T-572 and T-596 died in. Measured there:

`make gate` passed every one of these; the 80 s limit, the build-slot caps and
the shard count are untouched. The last row is the machine's own noise and is
quoted rather than hidden: the "before" gate started with the cold build on all
cores and the "after" gate started as that build ended, and the second ran
5 s slower. Two gates of one tree, minutes apart, differ by up to 16 s; the
stable number is the sum of test times, which is 140 s lower under the same
load after the change (435 → 295 s).

The 40 s target is met when the gate is the busiest thing on the machine
(30 s at 10 threads, against a cold build on every core); it is not met in the
4-thread corner (57 s), and cannot be: 2549 tests summing 295 s under load need
`295/NEXTEST_TEST_THREADS` seconds, so 40 s needs 8 threads or more, and the
caps that hand out fewer are not mine to change.

## Noted

- `#[ignore]` is the mechanism because the reason sits on the test: the gate's
  run skips it and `make full` (`scripts/test.sh --run-ignored all`) runs it.
  `make check` alone (one crate, a lane's light run) now skips them too, so a
  lane that edits `merge.rs` meets the merge tests at the gate or in
  `make full`, not in its own per-crate run. That is the cost of the change and
  it is on purpose.
- The machine's own noise is larger than the effect at gate level: two gate
  runs of the same tree, minutes apart, differed by 16 s (51 s and 67 s), driven
  by how many build-slot runs were alive and how much memory was free. That is
  why eight gate runs are quoted, and why the claim rests on the sum of test
  times (a quantity the machine's noise cannot flip).
- `crates/gear-store/tests/it/read_indexes_contract.rs` carries two unused
  imports (`SeatRole`, `TaskState`) — a warning from before this task, left
  alone.

## Not GEAR's to do

GEAR is a tool for other teams; it does not decide how much of a machine their builds and suites may use. On this machine, our own lanes' build and test cost is capped by this repo's Makefile (M4.2 T-238), which is our project's convention, not the product's. Dropped from the product backlog:
- **Concurrent suites can run the machine out of processes (M4 friction F-74, 07:20).** With several full suites at once, each forking git fixtures per test, a reviewer's run hit fork failures (per-user limit 2,666) and left an orphaned fixture wait. The M5 machine watch counts processes against `kern.maxprocperuid` and the engine holds a new suite (lane, reviewer or gate) while the count is high.
- **Builds are the largest friction (M4 friction reports, 05:30: ~35 of 58).** Lane and reviewer builds of 100-800 s under 4 lanes plus up to 4 reviewers, each in its own worktree target; a reviewer's fresh worktree builds cold (9 min). sccache hit 15%: of 4,618 calls, 2,846 were non-cacheable as `incremental` (our own crates), and with no `SCCACHE_BASEDIRS` two worktrees never share an entry. M5 measures the options against the 300 s red line: basedirs for the worktree root, incremental off for reviewer and merge builds only, a warm reviewer target reused across reviews, fewer concurrent full suites.
- One shared compile cache for all worktrees (was a sub-item of the machine health watch): a Rust-specific build choice of this repo, not GEAR's.

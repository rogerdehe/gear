## For M5 (user, 2026-10-26; regrouped 2026-10-28)
- **A captain that only relays (user, 2026-10-27, discuss at M5 kickoff).** Lanes think little so the captain must think: design, decide, write exact contracts, judge findings. Seen in another GEAR project: a captain that only splits messages into tasks, passes them on and acknowledges reports, deciding nothing. M5 settles how GEAR prevents and detects this.
- **A light procedure for small tasks (user, 2026-10-27, must).** Small, low-risk work must not go through the full loop (task, hand-in, review, rework, re-review). Example: adding two log lines to see a server error took that whole loop. Size the procedure to the work, decided from M4's per-round token and time data.
- Review per task, not only per project (a captain on another project, 2026-10-28): today `[temps]` switches review on for every hand-in or for none. A task that the compiler and its accept numbers fully check (deletions, a comment cut) needs no reviewer; one that changes test logic does. Part of the light procedure for small tasks: the plan names the tasks that skip review, and the captain settles them on their accept evidence.
- **Machine health watch (user, 2026-10-26 22:21: a long-term feature; "the ship must not break without us knowing").** M4 evidence: load 46 then 159 on 10 cores; two `find /` scans from an aborted lane turn ran 14+ min; four parallel builds plus another project's Go suite; fseventsd at 100% CPU and 3-4 GB for days; the user felt the lag before GEAR did. The engine samples every minute, as facts, not screen text:
  - load average vs cores, memory pressure, swap, free disk on the state and worktree volumes;
  - the processes under each seat's pane (by its shell pid): long-running children of a lane whose turn already ended (orphans, e.g. `find /`), builds and test runs per seat, and the top processes outside GEAR (named, never touched);
  - thresholds (kickoff-configurable): a captain item and a board line when load stays above 2× cores for 5 min, memory pressure is critical, disk is low, or an orphan of a lane runs over 10 min.
  - Actions GEAR may take on its own seats only: end an orphan of a finished lane turn; hold new dispatches while the machine is critical (no build lock: running work goes on). Never touch processes outside GEAR; name them to the captain, who tells the user.
  - Recorded in the voyage report: time spent above the thresholds and what caused it.
  - One shared compile cache for all worktrees (e.g. sccache), so lanes and reviewers do not each rebuild every dependency: M4 had eight `target/` dirs (~80 GB) and six full workspace builds at once (load 74 at two-to-four jobs each). Not a shared target dir: its build lock would serialize the lanes.
- **Concurrent suites can run the machine out of processes (M4 friction F-74, 07:20).** With several full suites at once, each forking git fixtures per test, a reviewer's run hit fork failures (per-user limit 2,666) and left an orphaned fixture wait. The M5 machine watch counts processes against `kern.maxprocperuid` and the engine holds a new suite (lane, reviewer or gate) while the count is high.
- **Builds are the largest friction (M4 friction reports, 05:30: ~35 of 58).** Lane and reviewer builds of 100-800 s under 4 lanes plus up to 4 reviewers, each in its own worktree target; a reviewer's fresh worktree builds cold (9 min). sccache hit 15%: of 4,618 calls, 2,846 were non-cacheable as `incremental` (our own crates), and with no `SCCACHE_BASEDIRS` two worktrees never share an entry. M5 measures the options against the 300 s red line: basedirs for the worktree root, incremental off for reviewer and merge builds only, a warm reviewer target reused across reviews, fewer concurrent full suites.
- Release lanes during a long wait (user, 2026-10-26 20:06; not urgent, saves resources): a kickoff option, asked once (`on_wait = "keep" | "release"`). With `release`, a lane parked on a quota stop (or another long declared wait) is released (agent exits, pane closed, worktree and session kept) and brought back at the reset: same worktree, its session resumed. Default `keep`.
- Stages of a long voyage on the board: a long voyage is cut into stations, each a release that runs end to end (M4 does this by hand as v0.4.0 … v0.4.4); the board shows the stations, which is reached, and what the next one needs. Design it from what M4's stages taught, not before.

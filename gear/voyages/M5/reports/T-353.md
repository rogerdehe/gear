## Attempt 2 — the review's F1 and F2

### F1: a wide glyph must not print its continuation

The review found that the terminal shifted every column after a voyage label:
`put_wide` wrote a real space as the glyph's second cell, so the fixture's
clock landed at terminal column 155 instead of the canvas's 152, `M4.2` at 21
instead of 20, and a fourth voyage's row ran to 161 columns on a 160-column
terminal. The golden gate compares stored cells, so it could not see it.

The canvas now keeps the prototype's columns and the *text* does not print a
continuation:

- `Canvas` holds `continuation: Vec<bool>`, one mark per cell.
- `Canvas::put_wide` writes the glyph, marks the next cell as its continuation
  (a space in the same style) and returns the cells it took; `put_styled` and
  `put_line` clear the mark of a cell they write, and `copy_rows` carries it,
  so the view's copy and the panel keep their marks.
- `Canvas::row_text` (the one path of `text()` and `ansi()`) leaves a marked
  cell's space out of the text, so a terminal draws exactly the canvas's
  columns; the stored cell is still a space with the label's style, which is
  what the golden `.txt`/`.col`/`.rev` planes read.
- `Canvas::is_continuation(x, y)` reads the mark.

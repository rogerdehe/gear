# T-288 — report

A lane that stays Working with no task while ready work waits is interrupted
after five minutes, once per condition, unless it is helping on an unanswered
captain instruction.

## What changed

**`crates/gear-engine/src/busy.rs`**

- `fn qualifies(tx, project, seat, obs) -> GearResult<bool>` (private): the
  condition `fn check` counted inline is now one function both monitors call —
  present, `AgentStatus::Working`, seat `Active`/`Standby`, the observation of
  the seat's current generation, no in-progress attempt, and a ready task whose
  attempts hold no seat. `check` keeps only the generation reset, the `:since`
  timer, and the alert; its behaviour at the threshold is unchanged.
- `pub fn due_interrupts(tx, project, observed, now) -> GearResult<Vec<String>>`:
  for every Lane observation `qualifies` accepts, whose `lane-busy-idle:<seat>:since`
  is set and at least 300 s old (`INTERRUPT_AFTER_S`), whose
  `lane-busy-idle:<seat>:interrupted` is not that same `since` value, and for
  which `gear_board::idle::helping(tx, project, &seat, now)?.is_some()` is false,
  it writes `:interrupted = since` and returns `get_binding(&seat.id, seat.generation)`'s
  `pane_id`. No predicate of its own: the condition is `qualifies`, "is helping"
  is T-323's `gear_board::idle::helping`.
  A lane with no binding for its generation is skipped and left unlatched, so a
  later tick with a binding still interrupts it.

**`crates/gear-engine/src/lib.rs`**

- In `fn tick`, right after `busy::check`:

      for pane in store.write(|tx| busy::due_interrupts(tx, &project, &observed, now))? {
          let _ = herdr.send_keys(&pane, &["esc".into()]);
      }

  A refused or uncertain `esc` is not retried: the latch is written before the
  key is sent, and the packet asks for no more.

## Tests (`crates/gear-engine/tests/it/busy_contract.rs`, same fixtures)

- `interrupts(&World)` counts the `Call::SendKeys { pane: LANE, keys: ["esc"] }`
  calls; `watch_interrupts` observes and runs `busy::check` + `busy::due_interrupts`
  as the tick does, for the test that makes the lane idle without dispatching.
- `working_with_no_task_is_interrupted_once_after_five_minutes`: no `esc` at
  0 s and 299 s, one at 300 s, still one at 301/400/601/1200 s, and the
  captain's busy item still opens (one item).
- `a_new_condition_after_recovery_is_interrupted_again`: one `esc` at 300 s,
  none at 301 s; the lane goes Idle (condition ends), Working again (new
  `:since`), none at 303 s and 602 s, one at 603 s — two `esc` in all.
- `a_lane_helping_on_an_unanswered_instruction_is_not_interrupted`: an unanswered
  captain `Instruction` to the lane → no `esc` at 0/300/900 s (even though the
  condition is far past 300 s); the lane's `Reply` (`reply_to` the instruction)
  ends the help, and the next tick sends one `esc`.
- The existing tests are untouched and still pass, including
  `working_with_only_submitted_attempts_alerts_once_after_ten_minutes` (the
  captain's alert at `timing.lane_busy_idle_min`), the overridden threshold and
  the store-reopen latch.

## Out of scope

- `esc` is the only key sent, at every profile (opencode, pi, codex): the packet
  fixes it. A provider whose TUI does not leave the turn on `esc` will not
  actually stop, and nothing reports that — no confirmation, no alert.
- A refused/uncertain `send_keys` is dropped silently (the latch is already
  written): the lane is not interrupted and never retried for that condition.
  Logging that belongs to the tick's report, which the packet does not extend.
- The `:interrupted` latch is never cleared on recovery, only compared against
  `:since`; that is the packet's rule, but it leaves one meta row per lane that
  lingers after the incident.
- Only lanes are interrupted; a reviewer busy with no task is not in the
  condition `fn check` counts and stays untouched.
- `cargo fmt --check` is not clean in this repo before my change
  (`crates/gear-app/src/admin.rs` and `gear-engine/src/busy.rs` itself are
  hand-formatted), so no reformat was run; my additions follow the file's
  existing style.
